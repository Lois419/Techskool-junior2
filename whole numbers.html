Whole Numbers

What is a Whole Number?

Whole numbers are all positive integers, beginning at zero and stretching to infinity. Decimals, fractions, and negative numbers aren't whole numbers. All whole numbers, except for zero, are also called natural numbers.

Counting

Mathematics likely began when people started to count and measure. Ancient people used fingers and toes to help them group numbers in different number bases such as 5s, 10s, and 20s. Base ten (denary system) is the most used today.

Roman Numerals were another system of counting:

  • I = 1
  • IV = 4
  • V = 5
  • IX = 9
  • X = 10
  • XX = 20
  • L = 50
  • C = 100
  • D = 500
  • M = 1000

Examples:
VI = 5 + 1 = 6
CD = 500 - 100 = 400
XXIV = 20 + 4 = 24

Place Value

Each digit in a number has a place value. For example: in 5834 → 5 (thousands), 8 (hundreds), 3 (tens), 4 (units).
